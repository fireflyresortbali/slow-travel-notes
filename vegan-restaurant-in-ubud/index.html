Where to Eat Vegan in Ubud: Inside Wild Vegan on Jalan Sugriwa

Ubud has no shortage of places to eat plants. Smoothie bowls, raw cakes and oat-milk lattes are on almost every corner of the town centre. What is harder to find is a kitchen that treats vegetables, roots and ferments as something more than a trend, and cooks them with the kind of attention you would normally give to meat.

That is the idea behind Wild Vegan, a small restaurant on Jalan Sugriwa, a quiet side street a short walk from the Monkey Forest road.

Food as medicine, without the lecture

The kitchen calls its cooking medicinal, which can sound like a warning that dinner will taste of good intentions. It doesn't. The starting point is Indonesian: turmeric, ginger, galangal, tamarind and the jamu tonics Balinese families have been brewing for generations. Those ingredients go into soups, dressings and drinks, and you notice them because they taste good, not because a menu tells you they are good for you.

Everything is 100% plant-based, organic where the island allows, and made from whole foods rather than packets. There is no meat, no pork and no alcohol in the cooking, which is why the restaurant is a regular stop for Muslim and Jewish travellers as well as vegans.

The burger people come back for

Ask returning guests what they order and most of them say the same thing: the Signature Burger. The bun is bright green, coloured with spirulina, and the patty is built to hold together and actually satisfy, which is where a lot of vegan burgers fail. It has become the dish the place is known for, and plenty of reviewers call it the best vegan burger they have had in Bali.

The rest of the menu is split in two. The cooked side has things like BBQ tofu with fire-grilled vegetables, tempeh steak, lentil bolognese and pumpkin soup with fresh bread. The raw side is lighter: collard green wraps, zucchini lasagna and big medicinal salads. Drinks are a reason to visit on their own, with house kombucha in flavours like mango and acai grape, cold-pressed juices and tamarind coolers.

Save room for cake. A lot of people leave with a box.

What to know before you go

Wild Vegan is open every day from 10:00 to 22:00 for lunch and dinner. It is not a breakfast spot, so plan it for after a morning walk or yoga class rather than before. Walk-ins are welcome, and groups can book a table on WhatsApp.

The address is Jl. Sugriwa No.16, Ubud. If you are staying in central Ubud it is an easy walk, and scooter parking is right outside.

Why it stands out

With more than 1,200 Google reviews at an average of 4.8, Wild Vegan is one of the most reviewed places to eat in the area, and the reviews tend to mention the same things: generous plates, friendly staff and food that leaves you feeling better than when you sat down.

If you are looking for a vegan restaurant in Ubud that takes flavour as seriously as health, it is worth putting on your list, especially if you have been craving a proper burger.
